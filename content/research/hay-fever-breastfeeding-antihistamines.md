# Hay fever while breastfeeding: antihistamines and nasal sprays

> **Status:** practical evidence note, not individual medical advice. Australian context. Check current product labels and ask a pharmacist/doctor when symptoms are severe, persistent, unusual, or medicines are being combined.

## Quick practical summary

For a breastfeeding adult with typical allergic rhinitis (hay fever), especially **sneezing and a runny nose**, non-sedating antihistamines and locally acting nasal treatments are commonly used.

### Loratadine

- **Loratadine 10 mg once daily** is a commonly used non-drowsy oral antihistamine for adults.
- Loratadine is one of the preferred oral antihistamines during breastfeeding because transfer into breast milk is low.
- Brand switching is not required. Products containing the same active ingredient and dose are pharmacologically the same medicine for this purpose.
- It is reasonable to use the same antihistamine through a hay-fever season if it remains effective and is tolerated; there is no routine requirement to rotate brands.
- Examples encountered in Australian pharmacies include **Demazin Allergy + Hayfever Relief (loratadine 10 mg)** and other generic loratadine 10 mg products. Always verify the active ingredient on the current packet because brand ranges can contain different medicines.

### Cetirizine

- **Cetirizine 10 mg once daily** is another preferred breastfeeding option.
- It is often labelled non-drowsy, but some people do experience sleepiness, so loratadine may be preferable when minimising sedation is important.

### Fexofenadine

- Fexofenadine has low expected exposure through breast milk and is generally considered compatible with breastfeeding, although loratadine and cetirizine have traditionally been the preferred choices because of the breastfeeding evidence base.

## Persistent nasal hay fever

For symptoms occurring most days, an **intranasal corticosteroid** can provide better overall control of allergic rhinitis than relying only on an oral antihistamine. Nasal corticosteroids act mainly in the nose and have low systemic exposure.

Correct technique matters: aim the nozzle slightly outward, away from the nasal septum, and use regularly as directed rather than expecting the full benefit from occasional doses.

## Ryaltris: olopatadine + mometasone

**Ryaltris** combines two medicines in one nasal spray:

- **Olopatadine 600 micrograms per actuation** — an intranasal antihistamine that can act relatively quickly on sneezing, itching and runny nose.
- **Mometasone furoate 25 micrograms per actuation** — an intranasal corticosteroid that suppresses the underlying allergic inflammation and works best with regular use.

An ePrescription describing “olopatadine 600 micrograms; mometasone furoate 25 micrograms nasal spray” and one describing **Ryaltris** may therefore refer to the same combination medicine rather than two different types of nasal spray. Confirm the dispensing details with the pharmacist.

### Breastfeeding and Ryaltris

- Intranasal mometasone has very low systemic absorption. Expert breastfeeding references consider nasal corticosteroids acceptable during breastfeeding, and clinically important infant exposure is not expected.
- Direct breastfeeding data for intranasal olopatadine are limited. Because nasal administration produces low maternal systemic exposure, large amounts in breast milk are not expected.
- The combination product's official information is necessarily more cautious because direct studies of the combination during breastfeeding are limited.
- When a clinician has prescribed Ryaltris, a pharmacist or prescriber can help decide whether it should be used instead of, rather than routinely stacked with, an oral antihistamine.

## Decongestants: important distinction

Do not assume every product sold under a familiar cold/allergy brand contains the same ingredients. Combination products may contain a **decongestant**.

**Oral pseudoephedrine can reduce milk production**, so it is generally best avoided when maintaining breastfeeding supply matters unless a clinician or pharmacist specifically recommends it. This warning does **not** apply to a plain loratadine-only product simply because another product from the same brand contains a decongestant.

## Choosing between options

For predominantly **sneezing and runny nose**, loratadine is a simple oral option. If symptoms are persistent or inadequately controlled, an intranasal corticosteroid or prescribed combination spray such as Ryaltris may provide stronger nasal symptom control. Avoid automatically combining multiple antihistamines; check with a pharmacist or prescriber first.

## Safety notes

Seek medical assessment if symptoms do not behave like ordinary hay fever, are severe or persistent despite appropriate treatment, or are accompanied by significant breathing difficulty, wheeze, facial swelling, fever or other concerning symptoms. Call **000** for a severe allergic reaction or life-threatening breathing difficulty.

When breastfeeding, consider the age and health of the child, milk supply, other medicines and maternal medical conditions. Advice for a healthy older breastfed child may not automatically apply to a premature or medically vulnerable infant.
