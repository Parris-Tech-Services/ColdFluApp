# Postinfectious dry cough — research protocol

> **Status:** AI-assisted discovery research only. Not clinically reviewed, not approved for publication, and not medical advice.
>
> **Review date:** 15 September 2026
>
> This package follows the repository research workflow. It must pass independent evidence, clinical, editorial and accessibility review before any claims are promoted to canonical reader content.

## Research question

For adults with a predominantly dry cough persisting after an acute respiratory infection, especially in the subacute 3–8 week window, what interventions safely reduce cough frequency/severity or sleep disruption, what underlying causes should be considered, and when should investigation escalate?

## Population

Adults with subacute or persistent predominantly dry cough after an apparent respiratory infection, without assuming a diagnosis. Evidence from chronic refractory/unexplained cough is included only where it informs cough-suppression techniques or differential diagnosis and is labelled as indirect.

## Outcomes

Priority outcomes:

1. cough frequency and severity;
2. nocturnal/morning symptoms and sleep disruption;
3. time to cough resolution;
4. quality of life;
5. adverse effects and clinically important interactions;
6. need for diagnostic testing or referral.

Comfort-only outcomes are kept separate from evidence that shortens illness or resolves the underlying cause.

## Interventions and causes in scope

- Natural history and postinfectious cough/cough-reflex hypersensitivity.
- Environmental modification: humidity, humidifiers, smoke, aerosols, fragrance, allergens and particulate matter.
- Hydration, honey, warm/cold fluids, lozenges/demulcents, steam/showers.
- Saline nasal spray/irrigation, intranasal corticosteroids, antihistamines and decongestants when upper-airway disease is plausible.
- Reflux lifestyle measures, alginates/antacids, H2 blockers and proton-pump inhibitors.
- Dextromethorphan, codeine, pholcodine regulatory status, benzonatate Australian availability, and specialist antitussives.
- Salbutamol, inhaled corticosteroids, ICS/LABA and ipratropium when airway disease is plausible.
- Behavioural cough suppression, speech pathology, breathing and laryngeal strategies.
- Pertussis and other alternative diagnoses where the clinical pattern warrants testing.
- Thresholds for spirometry, FeNO, chest radiography and specialist referral.

## Evidence priority

1. Australian government/regulator/professional guidance: Healthdirect, TGA, CICADA/TSANZ/Lung Foundation Australia, National Asthma Council/Australian Asthma Handbook.
2. International evidence-based cough guidelines: CHEST, ERS and NICE.
3. Cochrane/systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
4. Randomised controlled trials and major peer-reviewed reviews.
5. Manufacturer information only for device/product characteristics, never efficacy claims.

## Inclusion principles

- Current guidance was preferred where available.
- Older guidelines were retained where they contain influential recommendations, but newer syntheses take precedence when conclusions conflict.
- Australian regulatory/availability claims require Australian sources where possible.
- Evidence for chronic cough is not treated as direct proof for a 3–8 week postinfectious cough.

## Exclusion / caution principles

- Retailer and wellness claims are not efficacy evidence.
- Antibiotics are not treated as routine postviral-cough treatment.
- Paediatric honey/cough evidence is not automatically generalised to adults.
- Absence of a product from a web search is not treated as definitive proof of Australian non-availability; the ARTG is the authoritative register.
- This review does not provide individual dosing instructions or diagnose a person.

## Evidence interpretation

Claims are labelled broadly as:

- **Higher confidence:** supported by current guideline(s) and/or consistent higher-level evidence.
- **Moderate/conditional:** reasonable evidence but applicable only to a subgroup or with indirectness.
- **Low/uncertain:** small, inconsistent or old trials, or evidence extrapolated from a different cough population.
- **Not supported / avoid routinely:** evidence shows little benefit, or harms/regulatory issues outweigh likely benefit for undifferentiated cough.

## Important limitation

This is a targeted evidence review, not a formally registered systematic review. It does not claim exhaustive database coverage, duplicate screening or independent risk-of-bias adjudication. Independent clinical and evidence-methods review is still required.
