# Postinfectious dry cough — evidence gaps and review flags

> **Status:** AI-assisted draft; not clinically reviewed.

## High-priority evidence gaps

### 1. Week-by-week natural history

No robust adult postinfectious-cough dataset was found that supports reliable cumulative recovery percentages at weeks 3, 4, 6 and 8. The 3–8 week definition is useful clinically but should not be converted into an invented probability curve.

**Publication rule:** state the expected window and uncertainty; do not quote week-specific percentages unless a suitable prospective cohort is verified.

### 2. Behavioural cough suppression in subacute postinfectious cough

Strongest behavioural evidence is in refractory/unexplained **chronic** cough. Swallowing, sipping water, nasal breathing and slow exhalation are low-risk strategies, but whether they shorten a postinfectious cough or merely reduce bouts is unknown.

**Publication rule:** label as indirect evidence and symptom-control strategy, not a proven cure.

### 3. Humidifier withdrawal as treatment

Australian humidity guidance and ultrasonic-humidifier safety data support avoiding excess humidity and contamination, but no high-quality trial was found showing that stopping a humidifier improves postinfectious cough.

**Publication rule:** present an RH-guided humidifier-off trial as an environmental experiment when relevant, not as established therapy.

### 4. Adult honey evidence for postinfectious cough

Much honey evidence concerns children and acute upper-respiratory cough. Evidence specific to adult 3–8 week postinfectious cough is sparse.

**Publication rule:** classify honey as comfort/possible short-term symptom relief only.

### 5. Dextromethorphan effect size

Adult acute-cough trials are inconsistent and older; product registration confirms availability/indication, not magnitude of effect in postinfectious cough. Interaction risk also depends on concomitant medicines.

**Publication rule:** describe benefit as small/uncertain and temporary, require pharmacist/label interaction check, and avoid implying illness shortening.

### 6. Benzonatate Australian status

No routine Australian listing was identified in the targeted web search, but absence from search results is not definitive. The ARTG must be searched directly at publication review.

**Publication rule:** do not state simply “not available in Australia” without an explicit regulator search result.

### 7. New P2X3 / specialist antitussive regulatory status

CICADA 2024 stated that emerging cough-specific antitussives were not then approved in Australia. This is time-sensitive and could have changed by 2026.

**Publication rule:** re-check TGA/ARTG immediately before any current availability claim.

### 8. Intranasal treatments and cough outcome

Intranasal corticosteroids are established therapy for inflammatory rhinitis/rhinosinusitis, but cough-specific efficacy is weak. Saline improves rhinitis symptoms with low-certainty evidence; whether this translates to cough improvement depends on upper-airway disease actually being causal.

### 9. HEPA filtration

Trials show particulate reduction and some benefit in allergic rhinitis/asthma populations, not uncomplicated postviral cough.

**Publication rule:** reserve for allergy/particulate trigger context, not general cough advice.

### 10. Warm versus cold fluids

No high-quality evidence was found that fluid temperature changes duration of persistent cough.

**Publication rule:** recommend preference/comfort rather than a therapeutic temperature.

## Conflicting/older evidence requiring careful wording

### Ipratropium

Older ACCP guidance suggests inhaled ipratropium may help postinfectious cough, while newer syntheses conclude pharmacologic treatments overall lack convincing benefit. Do not elevate older ipratropium advice above the newer evidence base without reviewing the underlying trial(s).

### Empirical inhaled corticosteroids

Chronic-cough guidelines may support short, defined ICS trials in selected eosinophilic/asthmatic phenotypes, while undifferentiated postinfectious-cough reviews do not support routine ICS. The distinction between **treating diagnosed/suspected airway disease** and **treating postinfectious cough itself** must remain explicit.

### Reflux

Older reflux-cough literature encouraged broad empirical therapy; more recent CHEST/CICADA recommendations are more cautious, especially without heartburn/regurgitation or objective evidence. Current synthesis should follow the newer cause-directed approach.

## Safety-review flags before publication

A clinician/pharmacist should independently verify:

- current Australian scheduling and contraindications for dextromethorphan-containing products;
- serotonergic interactions and MAOI warnings for dextromethorphan;
- intranasal corticosteroid and antihistamine product-level cautions;
- current status of ipratropium and other inhaled therapies for cough indications;
- current ARTG status of benzonatate and newer antitussives;
- pholcodine recall/anaesthesia wording;
- red-flag wording and care-pathway localisation.

## Recommended next research steps

1. Retrieve and appraise the primary RCTs underlying the 2018 subacute-cough pharmacotherapy systematic review.
2. Search prospective cohorts specifically for adult postinfectious-cough time-to-resolution data.
3. Appraise adult acute-cough dextromethorphan trials and quantify absolute/clinical effect rather than relying on label indication.
4. Search Australian medicine PI/CMI for product-level interaction and scheduling details.
5. Repeat TGA/ARTG regulatory searches immediately before clinical review/publication.
6. Commission independent evidence-methods and respiratory/primary-care clinical review before creating a canonical module.
