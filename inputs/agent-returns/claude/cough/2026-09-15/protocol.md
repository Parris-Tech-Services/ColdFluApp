## Question

In adults, what interventions reduce a persistent, predominantly dry cough that continues after an
acute respiratory tract infection, and what is the expected natural history of that cough?

## Population, intervention, comparison, outcomes

- **Population:** adults with cough persisting beyond roughly three weeks after an acute respiratory
  tract infection, without red-flag features.
- **Interventions:** demulcents (honey, lozenges), over-the-counter antitussives, nasal saline,
  intranasal corticosteroids, antihistamines, decongestants, inhaled corticosteroids and
  bronchodilators, acid-suppressing medicines, behavioural cough-suppression therapy, and
  environmental measures.
- **Comparisons:** placebo, no treatment, or usual care.
- **Outcomes:** cough symptom relief, illness duration, harms, care escalation.

## In scope

Adults. Persistent dry cough after infection. Australian availability and regulatory status where
a medicine is discussed.

## Explicitly OUT of scope for this return

- Productive / wet cough (part of the module scope, not covered here).
- Children and infants.
- Cough in COPD, bronchiectasis, interstitial lung disease, or cancer.
- Smoking-related cough.
- Diagnostic triage of the underlying cause of cough.
- Dosing of any medicine.

Because the module scope is "dry and productive", **this return covers less than half of the module
and must not be treated as a complete draft.**

## Evidence hierarchy applied

1. Australian guidelines and Australian peer-reviewed clinical guidance
2. CHEST / American College of Chest Physicians cough guidelines
3. European Respiratory Society guidance
4. NICE
5. Cochrane reviews
6. Other systematic reviews and meta-analyses
7. Randomised controlled trials
8. Major peer-reviewed narrative reviews

## Deviations from the protocol

Substantial. See `search-log.md`. No bibliographic database was searched, no full texts were
obtained except one, and no risk-of-bias assessment was performed. This return is **discovery
material**, not a completed systematic search.
