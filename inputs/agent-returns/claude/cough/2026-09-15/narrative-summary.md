# Narrative summary - persistent dry cough after respiratory infection in adults

**Unverified discovery material. Not reviewed. Not health advice. Not canonical content.**

This is an extra file beyond the required bundle, included because it makes the reasoning behind
`module.json` legible to a human reviewer. It is de-identified and general. It must not be promoted
to `content/` and must not be published in any form.

## Natural history

Cough persisting after an acute respiratory infection is conventionally described as subacute
between three and eight weeks, and chronic beyond eight weeks in adults. Australian guidance defines
chronic cough as more than eight weeks in adults and more than four weeks in children. Post-infectious
cough is reported to affect roughly 11-25% of adults after a respiratory infection, and by definition
resolves within eight weeks, usually without treatment.

Precise adult resolution percentages at three, four, six and eight weeks **are not available** in the
literature located. The nearest Australian data come from a small primary-care series reporting that
78% were still coughing at one week and 35% at three weeks, with one patient coughing for ten weeks.
A larger analysis of cough-dominant respiratory infections found four recovery trajectories, and
found that the presence of lung disease was independently associated with slower recovery.

## What the evidence supports

- **Behavioural cough-suppression therapy** has the strongest randomised evidence of any
  intervention found, though in refractory chronic cough rather than the subacute window.
- **Honey** has moderate-certainty Cochrane evidence in children with acute cough, and one small
  adult trial in post-infectious cough specifically.
- **Demulcent lozenges and sprays** reduce throat pain; the effect on cough is less established, and
  the soothing vehicle may be the active component of cough mixtures generally.

## What the evidence does not support

- Over-the-counter cough mixtures generally.
- Codeine.
- Acid-suppressing medicines where there are no reflux symptoms.
- Antibiotics for viral cough, including where sputum is discoloured.
- Increased fluid intake beyond thirst, and steam inhalation, as duration-shortening measures.

## The most important caveat found

Trials of inhaled corticosteroids and other treatments for post-infectious cough have
**systematically excluded people with asthma**. The common conclusion that "no treatment works for
post-viral cough" therefore does not apply to readers with asthma, in whom a respiratory infection
can destabilise the underlying condition. Australian guidance names asthma as the well-known example
of a comorbid condition triggered by a respiratory infection causing cough to persist.

Any consumer-facing module must handle this carefully. A blanket reassurance that post-viral cough
needs no treatment could discourage a reader with asthma from having their asthma reviewed.

## Australian specifics

- Pholcodine products were cancelled and recalled in 2023 over anaphylaxis risk with neuromuscular
  blocking agents in general anaesthesia.
- Codeine has been prescription-only since February 2018.
- The Australian Asthma Handbook was updated in September 2025 to prioritise ICS-formoterol
  combination inhalers and recommend strongly against short-acting beta-2 agonist monotherapy.
- Australia recorded more than 57,000 pertussis cases in 2024, with early 2026 activity back to
  inter-seasonal levels.
