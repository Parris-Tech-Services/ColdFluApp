# Health learning resource library

**Status:** curated discovery library; links are not equivalent to clinical endorsement  
**Updated:** 15 September 2026  
**Audience:** households, carers, first aiders, students, nurses/aged-care workers and anyone wanting trustworthy Australian health learning resources.

This library is deliberately separate from canonical medical claims. A useful podcast or video can teach and orient; it does **not** replace current clinical guidelines or the repository's review process.

## How resources are selected

Prefer, in order:

1. Australian government/regulator/guideline bodies;
2. recognised Australian professional or health organisations;
3. systematic reviews and peer-reviewed studies;
4. research-based lived-experience resources;
5. reputable educational podcasts/videos with identifiable expert contributors.

Avoid influencer health content, supplement marketing, undated medical videos, anonymous medical advice and anything that presents anecdote as proof.

# Evidence literacy and lived experience

- [Healthtalk Australia](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/partners/healthtalk-australia) — research-based lived experience in audio/video/text.
- [ABC Health Report](https://www.abc.net.au/listen/programs/healthreport) — research news and interpretation.
- [Australian Prescriber](https://australianprescriber.tg.org.au/) — independent medicines/therapeutics publication.
- [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/) — systematic reviews.
- [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) — biomedical literature discovery.

Planned learning modules should teach:

- guideline vs systematic review vs RCT vs observational study;
- absolute vs relative risk;
- statistical significance vs meaningful benefit;
- publication bias and conflicts of interest;
- indirect evidence and population mismatch;
- why a plausible mechanism is not proof of benefit;
- why one positive study should not outrank a better evidence synthesis;
- why personal experience matters for care but is not proof of treatment effectiveness.

# Media backlog

For every major health collection, curate:

- [ ] 5–10 authoritative written references;
- [ ] 2–5 high-quality podcasts or audio explainers;
- [ ] 2–5 videos/animations where visual teaching materially helps;
- [ ] 1–3 guideline/systematic-review/landmark-paper links for deeper reading;
- [ ] a short quiz tied to the sources;
- [ ] an Australian escalation/helpline box where relevant;
- [ ] date checked, jurisdiction and source owner.

Do not auto-embed media in emergency flows. Learning media belongs in dedicated learning surfaces so it cannot delay first aid or urgent escalation.
