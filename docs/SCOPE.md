# Scope and module map

## Programme scope after 15 September 2026

The project is expanding from a cold-and-flu knowledge base into a broad Australian-first health reference. The expansion is governed by [HEALTH_TOPIC_MASTER_ROADMAP.md](HEALTH_TOPIC_MASTER_ROADMAP.md).

**Important:** scope expansion does not convert a topic into reviewed content. The existing 28-module respiratory catalogue remains the first controlled collection and should be completed under the current evidence/review system before large batches of new canonical modules are published.

## Required expansion before claiming broad usefulness

- Complete a reusable safety-critical triage foundation covering emergency, urgent, same-day and routine pathways.
- Add first aid/emergency modules before presenting the product as a workplace first-aider reference.
- Separate pregnancy from breastfeeding where recommendations differ.
- Add renal disease, liver disease, diabetes, anticoagulant use, polypharmacy and other medicine-safety overlays.
- Treat COVID-19 as a maintained topic rather than only a comparison row.
- Include medicine poisoning and accidental double-dose guidance by jurisdiction.
- Add child/family, mental health/neurodivergence, heart/circulation, sleep/recovery, nutrition, aged-care/nursing, workplace/environmental and digital-health collections.
- Add health-literacy, disability, carer, low-resource and accessibility perspectives.
- Define jurisdiction packs beyond Australia only when reviewed locally.
- Curate source-linked podcasts/videos/deeper reading separately from canonical health claims.
- Keep personal health-data analysis in companion tools such as HealthLens rather than adding personal records to this public knowledge base.

## Content boundaries

Each symptom/condition module may explain supportive care, common trajectories, relevant tests, prevention and escalation signals. It must not assert a diagnosis from symptoms alone.

Each intervention module must distinguish:

- prevention from treatment;
- adults from children and special populations;
- statistical findings from clinically meaningful benefit;
- studied formulation, route, dose and timing from unlike retail products;
- short-term use from repeated or long-term exposure;
- symptom relief from disease modification, complication prevention and mortality outcomes.

First-aid and triage content is safety-critical and must be independently reviewed, highly visible and localised. Emergency warnings must not depend on JavaScript, search, quiz completion, media playback or a questionnaire.

Nursing/aged-care/workplace content is educational unless an explicit product decision creates a clinically governed decision-support function. Local care plans, scope of practice and employer policy remain authoritative.

## Publication units

A “module” is a reader-facing topic. Within it, every evidence rating applies to an individual claim. Sources are reusable records referenced by claims. Jurisdiction and population overlays can amend — not silently overwrite — core content.

A broad roadmap item should remain a roadmap item until research actually begins; do not pre-create hundreds of empty canonical modules.
