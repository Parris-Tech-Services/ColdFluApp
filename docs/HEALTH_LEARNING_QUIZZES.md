# Health learning quiz bank

**Status:** educational draft; not a qualification, first-aid certificate or clinical competency assessment  
**Updated:** 15 September 2026

These short quizzes are designed to turn passive reading/listening into active recall. Questions deliberately test **principles and safety boundaries**, not personalised diagnosis or medication dosing.

For first aid, nursing or workplace use, current accredited training, employer policy, scope of practice and authoritative guidelines always outrank this quiz bank.

---

## Quiz 1 — DRSABCD, CPR and AED

1. When an unresponsive person is not breathing normally, should you delay attaching an AED until several minutes of CPR have passed?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No
2. Which organisation publishes the core Australian/New Zealand resuscitation guidelines used in this project?
   - A. ANZCOR
   - B. TGA
   - C. PBS
3. Does an AED replace CPR?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No — CPR and AED are parts of the same response

<details><summary>Answers and learning links</summary>

1. **B.** Use an AED as soon as it is available; do not create an arbitrary delay.  
2. **A. ANZCOR.**  
3. **B.** Continue to follow CPR/AED prompts and emergency-service instructions.

Read: [ANZCOR CPR](https://www.anzcor.org/home/basic-life-support/guideline-8-cardiopulmonary-resuscitation-cpr) · [ANZCOR AED](https://www.anzcor.org/home/basic-life-support/guideline-7-automated-external-defibrillation-in-basic-life-support)

</details>

## Quiz 2 — Asthma, anaphylaxis and heart-attack emergencies

1. Should severe breathing difficulty that is not responding to an asthma reliever be managed only by waiting at home?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No
2. Which medicine class is central to first aid for anaphylaxis?
   - A. Adrenaline
   - B. Antibiotics
   - C. Antacids
3. If heart-attack warning signs are severe or persistent, which Australian number should be called for an ambulance?
   - A. 000
   - B. 13 11 26
   - C. 1800 022 222

<details><summary>Answers and learning links</summary>

1. **B.** Severe or poorly responsive asthma needs urgent escalation according to the person's action plan/first-aid guidance.  
2. **A. Adrenaline.**  
3. **A. 000.**

Read/watch: [Asthma Australia first aid](https://asthma.org.au/medicines-treatment/asthma-first-aid/) · [ANZCOR anaphylaxis](https://www.anzcor.org/home/first-aid/guideline-9-2-7-first-aid-management-of-anaphylaxis) · [Heart Foundation warning-sign videos/action plan](https://www.heartfoundation.org.au/your-heart/heart-attack-warning-signs)

## Quiz 3 — Colds, cough and respiratory illness

1. A cough after a respiratory infection can sometimes persist after the initial infection settles. True or false?
2. Does every persistent cough need antibiotics?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No
3. If a cough reaches the chronic-cough time range or develops red flags, is further assessment more appropriate than simply adding more home remedies?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No

<details><summary>Answers and learning links</summary>

1. **True.** Post-infectious cough is a recognised subacute pattern.  
2. **B.** Antibiotics only help when there is an appropriate bacterial indication.  
3. **A.** Persistence and red flags change the assessment pathway.

Read: [CICADA Australian cough statement](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2024/220/1/cough-children-and-adults-diagnosis-assessment-and-management-cicada-summary) · research package `content/research/postinfectious-dry-cough/`.

## Quiz 7 — HRV, recovery and wearables

1. Does one low HRV reading prove that you are sick or overtrained?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No
2. Which is usually more meaningful for HRV interpretation?
   - A. A personal trend measured consistently
   - B. Comparing one reading with a stranger's value
3. Can alcohol, illness, exercise load, breathing pattern, sleep and measurement artefact all affect HRV?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No

<details><summary>Answers and learning links</summary>

1. **B.** HRV is variable and nonspecific.  
2. **A.** Consistent personal trends are more interpretable than isolated cross-person comparisons.  
3. **A.** Many physiological and technical factors alter HRV.

Use **HealthLens** for private personal-data analysis: <https://github.com/joshualparris/HealthLens>. The public reference app should teach measurement limits rather than infer personal diagnoses.

</details>

## Quiz 18 — Evidence and health-information literacy

1. Does a plausible biological mechanism prove that a treatment works in people?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No
2. If an RCT and a later high-quality systematic review disagree, should the review usually be examined carefully before promoting the single trial?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No
3. Does “statistically significant” automatically mean the benefit is large enough to matter clinically?
   - A. Yes
   - B. No

<details><summary>Answers and learning links</summary>

1. **B.** Plausibility is not clinical-effectiveness evidence.  
2. **A.** Evidence synthesis can provide broader context, although review quality still matters.  
3. **B.** Effect size, confidence intervals, harms and clinical relevance matter.

Explore: [Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) · [Australian Prescriber](https://australianprescriber.tg.org.au/) · [Healthtalk Australia](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/partners/healthtalk-australia).
